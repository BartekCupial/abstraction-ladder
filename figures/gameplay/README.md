# Recorded Medusa encounter

The default example is adapted from an actual frame in [BotHack’s Samurai ascension recording](https://github.com/krajj7/BotHack/blob/aa6a93c8993d92fa0a4c7a02ab529f241639c110/ttyrec/samurai-bot-asc.ttyrec.xz), credited to [BotHack and its contributors](https://github.com/krajj7/BotHack). It shows Sambot beside Medusa when the game reports that her gaze is reflected by the player’s shield. Water is added from the matching wiki layout to reveal the islands’ outlines. BotHack controls the character in this recording. This is not an episode from our paper’s experiments.

The captured frame is record 1,020,257, counted from zero, at 2h 29m 32.430069s from the beginning of the recording. The source commit is `aa6a93c8993d92fa0a4c7a02ab529f241639c110`. The pinned URL, timestamp, byte offsets, and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in `sources.json`.

`medusa-encounter.ttyrec` contains the eight original terminal records from the last full redraw through this frame. Replaying this excerpt with an 80-column, 24-row terminal using `pyte==0.8.2` produced `medusa-encounter.html`. A replay beginning at the preceding full redraw was checked to produce identical cells and colors. The original recording is available at the linked source.

The HTML fragment preserves every displayed character and ANSI foreground color. Its first row is the original game message and its last two rows are the original player stats. Rows 1 through 21 form the level. Their unused final column is checked to be blank before trimming to the shared 79 by 21 viewport.

The capture labels two existing glyphs so they receive the appropriate graphics. Using zero-based coordinates within the level, the white `@` at `(70, 11)` is the player, confirmed by the terminal cursor. The adjacent bright green `@` at `(69, 11)` is Medusa, confirmed by the gaze message and subsequent records in which she becomes a statue. Blue `~` cells are labeled as water to account for this recording’s display settings. The archived capture remains unchanged. The graphical viewer uses the same simplified vector tiles as the other examples, including a distinctive Medusa tile and name label.

The added water comes from the [second Medusa layout](https://nethackwiki.com/wiki/Medusa%27s_Island#Second_version), archived in `../wiki/medusa-2.html` at wiki revision 209604. Its coordinates are shifted two columns right and one row down to align with the recorded stairs, walls, and visible water. Only water glyphs can fill blank cells in the capture. The generator stores these cells in a separate `terrainReveal` layer, preserving the original recording’s positions, creatures, message, and stats. The page caption and water tooltips identify the addition. Other unexplored terrain remains blank. See the [wiki attribution and adaptation notes](../wiki/README.md) for the layout source and its licenses.

Run `python scripts/extract-wiki-maps.py` to regenerate the viewer data from the archived HTML with Python’s standard library. Replaying the recording is not needed for a normal build.
